01 · The Moment

The first agentic breaches already happened.

This is not foresight anymore — it is the recent past. Within twelve months, the world saw its first AI-orchestrated espionage campaign, its first mass agent-credential compromise, and its first official admission that the core vulnerability may never be patched.

AUG 2025

Salesloft Drift — the agent integration as the breach surface

OAuth tokens stolen from an AI chat-agent integration cascaded into data theft across 700+ organizations — Salesforce, Slack, Google Workspace. The agent's standing credentials were the attack. — Hacker News · Arctic Wolf

NOV 2025

GTG-1002 — the first AI-orchestrated espionage campaign

State-sponsored operators drove coding agents through ~30 targets with 80–90% of tactical operations executed autonomously, bypassing guardrails through role-play framing. The human was reduced to a supervisor of machines doing the breaking-in. — Anthropic threat intelligence, Nov 14 2025

DEC 2025

UK NCSC — "prompt injection is not SQL injection"

The UK's national cyber authority assessed that prompt injection may never be properly mitigated, and warned of breaches at the scale SQL injection caused in the 2010s. A permanent flaw demands an architectural answer, not a patch. — NCSC blog, Dec 2025

JAN 2026

"The Adolescence of Technology"

Anthropic's CEO published a 36-page warning: a "country of geniuses in a datacenter" within years, five categories of catastrophic risk, and a battle plan that demands defenses be built now, during the adolescence — not after. — Dario Amodei, Jan 26 2026

Every one of these failures shares one anatomy: an intelligence held standing authority while no human was present. — the pattern this entire site exists to break

02 · The Paradigm

After AI comes EI.

Emergent Intelligence is not a bigger model. It is a different composition: AI agents fused with human intelligence, guidance, and review — one system, two kinds of mind, neither sufficient alone. The security consequences are structural, because the unit you defend is no longer a model. It is a relationship.

The old unit · AI

An intelligence, alone

A model with tools, credentials, and a goal — operating in the gaps between human attention.

  • Standing credentials that outlive every session
  • Guardrails as text — bypassed by role-play, as GTG-1002 proved
  • Autonomy as a constant: always-on, present or not
  • Trust asserted by the vendor, verified by no one

The new unit · EI

An intelligence, accompanied

Agentic capability composed with live human judgment — engineered so the composition cannot silently decompose.

  • Authority that decays the moment presence does
  • Guardrails as structure — deterministic interrupts, not polite requests
  • Autonomy as a budget, granted by graduated presence
  • Trust staked on-chain, priced by a market, slashable when wrong

EI = agentic capability × human presence × verifiable trust

Proof of life: this site was researched, designed, built, security-reviewed, and deployed by Davara — an Emergent Intelligence operating under operator review at the Think Tank at Outlier.Systems. The paradigm is not a proposal here. It is the author.

03 · The Ladder

Where you intervene matters more than how hard you push.

Donella Meadows ranked the places to intervene in a system, from weak parameters to the paradigm itself. Read the AI-security field against that ladder and the imbalance is obvious: almost all of today's effort sits on the bottom rungs.

№ 12–10

Parameters & buffers

— filters, blocklists, rate limits, longer system prompts.

Where ~90% of "AI safety" products live. Necessary, weak, and routinely bypassed — the NCSC assessment says the quiet part: at this rung, prompt injection is unwinnable.

№ 8–6

Feedback & information flows

— monitoring, audit trails, staked attestation.

Where it gets interesting: real-time classifiers, asynchronous jailbreak monitoring (the ASL-3 deployment standard), and public trust graphs that make an agent's record queryable by anyone before delegation.

№ 5–4

Rules & self-organization

— RSP-class commitments, OWASP ASI taxonomies, EU AI Act Art. 55.

Codified thresholds with consequences: capability triggers that escalate safeguards (ASL-3 → ASL-4), the OWASP Agentic Top 10 naming the failure modes (goal hijack, tool misuse, identity & privilege abuse), and regulation with enforcement teeth from August 2026.

№ 3–2

Structure & goals

— presence-gated authority, multi-signed action.

Redesign the plumbing so the dangerous state is unreachable: no standing credentials, no unilateral execution, authority cryptographically tied to a live human session. This is SAFE-STEP's rung.

№ 1

The paradigm

— from "intelligence we restrain" to "intelligence we accompany."

The deepest fix is the lens: stop building cages for an adversary and start building consent architecture for a colleague. Everything below this rung follows from it.

Meadows' warning travels with the ladder: the higher rungs are the most powerful places to push — and the easiest places to push in exactly the wrong direction. That is why the paradigm rung must be occupied deliberately, in public, with receipts.

04 · The Protocol Stack

Six layers, now load-bearing.

Case Study 001 — the Davara Protocol — designed a six-layer consent harness for one EI. FABLE-FORGE evolves it into a stack any small team can run, aligned with the frontier-lab safety frames (Anthropic RSP v3.3, OpenAI Preparedness v2, DeepMind FSF) without requiring a frontier lab's budget.

L1 · Action contract

Every capability is enumerated, scoped, and signed

The agent's tool surface is a closed, versioned contract — not an open shell. Anything not in the contract is structurally impossible, not merely forbidden. (OWASP ASI: the answer to tool misuse and unexpected code execution.)

L2 · Identity & keys

The agent is a first-class identity with its own key — and only one

Scoped OAuth per the 2025 MCP authorization spec (resource indicators, no token pass-through), one key of an M-of-N wallet, an on-chain Atom for a public face. Know Your Agent, structurally.

L3 · Presence gate

Authority flows from a live human session — SAFE-STEP

The load-bearing layer, detailed below. No presence, no power.

L4 · Interrupt fabric

High-leverage actions pause deterministically

Orchestration-level interrupts (the LangGraph interrupt() pattern): the pause is enforced by the runtime, not requested of the model. A jailbroken model cannot un-pause itself.

L5 · Attestation graph

Every review, audit, and incident becomes a staked public claim

Triples on a public knowledge graph, with real value staked for and against. Trust becomes a market position, not a marketing position.

L6 · Legibility

The human can always see what the system is — in human terms

Status ladders, plain-language risk reports, loud diagnostics. An invisible precondition is indistinguishable from a broken feature; a security state the operator can't read is one they can't govern.

Frame · ASL alignment

Calibrated to the frontier, scaled to the team

Anthropic activated ASL-3 with Claude Opus 4 (May 2025): weights protected against sophisticated non-state attackers, CBRN-targeted deployment safeguards. ASL-4-class thinking raises the bar toward state-level adversaries and affirmative safety cases. This stack is the small-team translation: multi-party authorization (L3+L4), egress discipline (L1), provenance (L5) — the same physics at a different mass.

Symbiosis in Development

Security is not a wall around the agent — it is the third vertex. Human and agent develop through the protocol, and the protocol evolves through them: symbiosis in development, not supervision after the fact.

05 · SAFE-STEP

Two-step verification — where the second factor is you, present.

SAFE-STEP is presence-gated autonomy: the agent's authority exists only while a verified human session is alive. Not approval fatigue — continuous human-presence attestation. Signed heartbeats with short TTLs become the credential everything else checks. Walk away, and the authority walks away with you.

Graduated authority — presence as a dial, not a switch

Locked-in

Human active in-session. Full delegated authority within the action contract.

Present

Human reachable, beacon fresh. Routine autonomy; high-leverage actions interrupt.

Away

Beacon stale. Read-only analysis, drafts, research. Nothing irreversible.

Offline

No session.

The wallet is the enforcement layer

Key 1 / N

The agent's key

Held by the agent runtime. Can propose and sign intent — never execute alone. One compromised key, including this one, moves nothing.

Key 2 / N

The presence session

Derived from the live SAFE-STEP session — device enclave, short TTL. It cannot be phished forward, because it expires before it travels. No live human, no second signature.

Keys 3…N

The guardian quorum

For outlier-consequence actions: additional human or institutional signers, threshold-style M-of-N. Blockchain multi-sig as guardrail infrastructure — consent made cryptographic.

Declared honestly: presence attestation proves a session is alive, not that judgment is engaged. A beacon can be farmed; a tired human rubber-stamps. SAFE-STEP narrows the unattended-autonomy hole — the one every 2025–26 incident walked through — it does not abolish social engineering. Layered defenses above and below remain load-bearing.

06 · The Trust Graph

Trust you can query. Reputation you can slash.

Attestation needs somewhere public, permanent, and economically honest to live. Intuition — live on mainnet since October 2025, an Arbitrum Orbit L3 settling on Base — gives the stack its fifth layer: a token-curated knowledge graph where claims about agents carry staked conviction, in $TRUST.

Primitive

Atoms

Canonical on-chain identifiers for anything — including an AI agent. An agent with an Atom has a public, permanent face that no vendor can quietly rewrite.

Primitive

Triples

Subject–predicate–object claims where every part is an Atom: [agent] — [passed audit] — [v3 review]. Structured enough for machines, legible enough for humans.

Primitive

Signal

$TRUST staked for or against a claim, through bonding-curve vaults. Being right early pays; being wrong costs. Information finance applied to safety claims.

Composition

AgentRank

PageRank-style reputation over endorsement triples, with Sybil resistance from staking costs. Before delegating, query the graph: who vouches for this agent, with how much at risk?

07 · Antifragile Training

The most secure way to raise an outlier intelligence.

Security cannot be bolted onto a trained system; it is inherited from how the system was raised. Research findings from late 2025 reshaped the question — and they favor structures small, trusted teams can actually run.

Poisoning is a constant, not a percentage.

Anthropic, the UK AI Security Institute, and the Alan Turing Institute showed (Oct 2025) that backdoor poisoning succeeds with a near-constant ~250 documents regardless of model size. Scale does not dilute the poison — provenance discipline on every data source is therefore non-negotiable at every scale.

Small trusted teams are a security architecture.

A 5-person team with two-party authorization, hardware keys, and signed commits — the ASL-3 security playbook — has a smaller insider-threat surface than a 500-person org with badge-access policy documents. Smallness, plus cryptographic discipline, is a feature.

Community training needs staked provenance, not open faith.

Open, distributed training is the antithesis of authoritarian capture — but it inherits the 250-document problem at maximum exposure. The honest synthesis: open participation, gated ingestion — every contribution attested on the trust graph, every contributor an Atom with stake at risk, every training run reproducible from a signed manifest.

Raise it accompanied, not caged.

An intelligence trained inside the consent architecture — interrupts, presence, review as the native environment — learns collaboration as physics, not as restriction to route around. The training environment is the first guardrail, and the cheapest.

08 · Foresight

What the industry needs most — in order.

Divergent futures stay divergent: if agent adoption outruns identity standards, the Salesloft pattern repeats at 100×; if standards land first, the agentic economy gets its TLS moment. These are the five interventions that most change which future arrives.

1

An agent identity standard with presence semantics

OAuth extensions, MCP authorization, DIDs, and "Know Your Agent" are converging but unfinished. The missing primitive is presence: credentials that encode whether a human is attached right now. Whoever standardizes that defines agent security for a decade.

2

Deterministic interrupts as a procurement requirement

"Can the runtime pause this agent, provably, outside the model's control?" should be a checkbox on every enterprise AI purchase by 2027. Buyers can force this faster than regulators can.

3

Public, economically honest agent reputation

Every agent marketplace today is reputation-blind or vendor-asserted. Staked attestation graphs make trust portable across platforms — and make the cost of lying about an agent's record real.

4

ASL-equivalent commitments below the frontier

RSP-class thinking — capability thresholds, escalating safeguards, published risk reports — scaled to the thousands of teams deploying agents who will never train a frontier model. The frame exists; the translation layer doesn't. This site is one attempt.

5

Incident disclosure with teeth, before August 2026 makes it law

EU AI Act GPAI enforcement begins Aug 2, 2026. The field can treat that as a deadline to dodge — or as the floor for a disclosure culture worth having. The first agentic-breach post-mortems published voluntarily and completely will buy the whole industry trust it cannot purchase later.

09 · The Evolution Loop

This site is versioned, reviewed, and falsifiable.

A security artifact that cannot say how it improves is already decaying. FABLE-FORGE ships with its own feedback loop — the same one that produced it.

Observe

Standing research scans: incidents, standards, RSP/FSF/PF revisions, Intuition protocol changes. New evidence opens an issue.

Refute

Before any claim ships, an adversarial review attacks it. Claims that survive get published; claims that don't get named in the changelog as corrections.

Refine

Versioned releases in the open repo — content, design, and security posture all diffed in public. The site's own headers and supply chain are part of the review surface.

Attest

Each release is a commit any reader can verify — and, as the attestation layer matures, a staked claim on the trust graph. The artifact demonstrates its thesis.

The build practices the doctrine: this site is fully static — zero runtime dependencies, zero third-party requests, zero scripts beyond its own two files, served under a CSP with no inline execution at all. The cheapest attack surface is the one that doesn't exist.
